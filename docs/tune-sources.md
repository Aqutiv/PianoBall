## Performance and playable parts

Every track has explicit `Tune.backingNotes` for the automatic performance.
Melody mode plays these notes with their authored register, gain, attack and
sounding length. Backing mode instead plays only the corrected melody; the
player owns the independent compact accompaniment. Performance annotations do
not change graded hold lengths or keyboard fitting.

Composed classical arrangements use the struck-piano family for both parts.
Frère Jacques and Drunken Sailor retain music-box, choir, harp and guitar colors;
Hopscotch retains electric piano, compact felt-piano backing and its authored drums.
The retired Drift arrangement retains glass in the historical source notes. Specific voice IDs and exact tempos are exported from the
catalog, avoiding a separate manifest that could drift from the score.

Backing lessons retain the existing25-key fit, maximum three sounding notes,
one-octave simultaneous reach and at least220ms between distinct onsets.
Für Elise uses compact broken gestures and its written silences. Gymnopédie
releases the playable bass before the three-note beat-two chord. Minuet keeps
the moving source bass with compact initial support. The other lessons retain
their bass, chord-answer, compound-pulse or sustained-harmony teaching patterns.
Automatic parts may exceed those player limits.

## Progress and verification

The current track IDs, role IDs, ordering and earned access remain stable,
including the upstream Drift-to-Hopscotch replacement. Changed playable
charts have fresh bests in isolated `playtune.musicality.v2` and
`playbacking.musicality.v2` storage generations; previous results are visible
as historical arrangements. A historical and current pass count once per track.
Accompaniment-only edits preserve current bests. Old tabs cannot overwrite the
new generation, and current-tab resets clear both current and historical scores.
The migration lists are checked against playable signatures captured from
`9840971` for retained tracks. Hopscotch is explicitly a new track, and Drift is
retired; its best never becomes a Hopscotch result. Migration accepts both the
upstream progress stores and the prior musicality generation.

Targeted source regressions live in `tests/musical-classics.test.ts` and
`tests/musical-familiar.test.ts`; course constraints remain in the existing
playability suites. See [music-listening-review.md](music-listening-review.md)
for reproducible production-synth renders, objective checks and the explicitly
recorded scope of listening acceptance.

## September rhythm and traditional additions

The complete source readings are recorded in [Yankee Doodle and The Irish Washerwoman](traditional-marching-jig.md) and [La Bamba](traditional-la-bamba.md). These notes identify the exact passages, source-to-chart pitches, pickups, repeated forms, beat units, reductions and synthetic percussion choices. La Bamba uses the traditional lead line from the official SEP/ConArte songbook; this is a documented substitution for the planned 1939 recording transcription, which was not audibly verified in this environment. No recording or modern second voice/accompaniment is included.

Yankee Doodle and La Bamba are slower than the initial proposal so their source sixteenths remain at least 220 ms apart; the written rhythm is preserved. Yankee Doodle’s selected printed Theme has no repeat signs. Yankee Doodle and La Bamba use eighth-note chart units to keep the visible approach readable; La Bamba’s additional practice slowdown keeps its densest passage within the existing eight-onset limit. The Irish Washerwoman retains its initial two-sixteenth pickup, later pickups and shortened repeat bars.

First Light adds a soft quarter-note rim pulse with a slightly stronger downbeat. Two Hands adds quiet kick on one/three, rim on two/four and eighth-note hi-hat. Ground adds soft kick on one/three and quarter-note hi-hat. Off the Beat adds kick on one/three, rim on two/four and quarter-note hi-hat. Hopscotch’s authored parts, phrase changes and ending remain unchanged. The percussion additions leave graded notes untouched; the separate pitched corrections are documented above. None of the 32 existing classical/traditional role entries gains percussion.

The three new pieces use restrained synthetic marching or dance accents as editorial arrangements. They do not claim a historically performed drum part. Every new playable part uses piano; percussion remains an optional live layer shared across both roles, while the exported score always retains its authored drum events.
